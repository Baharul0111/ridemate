# RideMate — Goal

## The goal
RideMate is an Android app that makes booking a ride on Rapido, Uber and Ola quick, cheap and penalty-free. You tell it where you're going once. It opens each ride app you chose with your route ready, and with one tap on a small floating button it reads the prices on that app's screen into one simple list, such as "Auto: Ola ₹100 · Rapido ₹110 · Uber ₹120". It checks every number twice, in two independent ways. If it can't be sure of a price, it says so instead of guessing.

You book in the apps yourself. RideMate never taps, types or acts inside Rapido, Uber or Ola, and it never runs anything hidden. After you book, it watches those apps' notifications. The moment a driver accepts in one, it tells you how far away the driver is and asks "Cancel the others?" When you say yes, it opens each other app straight away, shows exactly where to tap Cancel, checks that each cancel really happened, and keeps reminding you until every extra booking is gone. So you never pay a penalty for a booking you forgot.

If you choose to keep looking and a much closer driver turns up, it shows you the distance and time and changes nothing unless you say yes. On the government's open ride network (ONDC) it can find, book and cancel a ride entirely by itself, after your tap. During the hackathon this runs against a clearly labelled demo seller. At the end of the ride it opens the ride app so you pay there.

It uses Meta's open-weights AI model to read screens, understand what you said and make sense of unfamiliar notifications. That model, and RideMate's small server, run on Modal, paid only from the $100 Modal hackathon credit. Baharul pays nothing out of his own pocket: there's no card anywhere, and every other part uses free tools. The server keeps keys safe, stops abuse, and stops AI spending before the credit runs out. Everything still works sensibly when the AI is unavailable or the AI budget runs out. Anyone without Indian ride apps, including hackathon judges, can try the whole experience in a clearly marked demo mode.

## What it must do
1. **Enter a trip once.** You type your destination with address suggestions, or say it aloud in a language your phone's voice typing supports (such as Hindi, English, Bengali or Assamese). Pickup defaults to where you are. You confirm the address before anything happens, and can view it in your phone's own maps app.
2. **Pick apps and vehicle types.** You choose which installed ride apps (Uber, Ola, Rapido) and which vehicle types (bike, auto, cab) to compare, and optionally the open ride network.
3. **Open each app with the route ready.** RideMate opens Uber with pickup and drop already filled in. For Ola and Rapido it opens the app and offers your drop address to paste with one tap.
4. **Read prices with one tap.** On each app's price screen you tap RideMate's floating button. It reads that screen and adds each option to its list. It only looks at your screen when you tap, and Android asks your permission for each comparison.
5. **Never show a wrong price.** A price, ETA or distance appears only when two separate readers agree on it: Meta's AI, and a reader built into your phone. Crossed-out "old" prices are ignored. If the readers disagree, the row says "Couldn't read — check in app" with no number.
6. **Show freshness.** Every price shows the time it was seen, and greys out after 60 seconds with a one-tap refresh.
7. **One clear list.** Prices are grouped by vehicle type with the cheapest verified option marked. Unsure rows are never marked cheapest.
8. **You book yourself.** You tap Book inside each ride app you want. RideMate records that you booked there.
9. **Watch the ride apps' notifications.** RideMate reacts to every notification from Uber, Ola and Rapido within a few seconds and ignores all other apps. If a notification is hidden by Android or worded in a way it doesn't recognise, it asks you a simple question ("Rapido has an update. Got a driver?") instead of staying silent.
10. **Driver found.** When the first driver accepts, RideMate shows how far away the driver is and how long they'll take (or says "see in Rapido" if it can't confirm that), plus whether cancelling your other bookings is likely to cost anything. If two drivers accept at almost the same time, it lets you pick. It never picks for you.
11. **"Cancel the others?"** When you say yes, RideMate opens each other app one after another, searching ones first because those are usually free to cancel. It shows that app's exact cancel steps, and can point an arrow next to the Cancel button if you ask. You tap Cancel yourself.
12. **Prove every cancel.** RideMate counts a booking as cancelled only when it sees proof: the app's own cancelled notification, or the ride notification disappearing along with a cancelled screen. If you say "I cancelled it", it checks again a minute later and warns you loudly if the ride still looks active.
13. **Keep reminding.** Until every extra booking is proven cancelled, ended by the app, or dismissed by you, RideMate reminds you after 30 seconds, 1 minute and 2 minutes, then every 2 minutes, even if you switch apps or close RideMate.
14. **Handle the ride apps' own changes.** If a driver cancels on you, a new driver is assigned, or a search expires, RideMate notices and updates. It withdraws any question that no longer makes sense, stops reminders for bookings the app already ended, and warns you loudly if the ride you kept is lost ("Your Rapido captain cancelled — compare again?").
15. **Spot the same driver.** If the same driver shows up on two apps, RideMate tells you to keep only one.
16. **Better ride, only with your yes.** If you turned on "keep searching" (off by default, with a fair-play warning) and a much closer driver appears, RideMate shows both drivers' distance and time and any cancellation cost. It switches nothing unless you tap Yes, and then guides you through cancelling the old one.
17. **Open ride network.** RideMate can search, book and cancel rides on ONDC by itself, every step after your tap. It shows any cancellation fee before you confirm. During the hackathon this uses a demo seller labelled as such.
18. **Pay in the original app.** When the ride ends, RideMate opens that ride app so you pay there. RideMate never handles money.
19. **Try the demo.** A clearly marked demo mode uses made-up sample screens from fictional ride companies. It shows reading prices, a driver being found, the cancel guide and a better-ride offer, without needing any ride app, sign-in or AI budget.
20. **Easy, honest setup.** First run walks you through an 18+ check, a plain privacy notice, Google sign-in (skippable; without it you type prices yourself), and each permission with a reason. On phones that need it, it guides "Allow restricted settings" and battery settings. A quick self-test confirms RideMate can see notifications. Before each comparison it checks that its permissions are still on.
21. **Works when things fail.** If the AI is slow or down, it tries once more, then lets you type the price. If the AI budget is used up, you're signed out, or you're offline, it lets you type prices too, and the notification guardian and cancel reminders keep working. It never hangs: every step has a time limit.
22. **Fix itself without an app update.** The owner can update how each ride app's screens and notifications are understood, and switch any feature off, remotely within minutes. A developer tool helps propose those fixes when a ride app changes, and the owner approves them.
23. **Never cost Baharul money.** Only the Modal credit is ever spent. AI features need Google sign-in, fair-use limits apply per person and per network, and new accounts get a smaller allowance on day one. RideMate meters everything it spends on Modal (AI and its own server), stops well before the credit runs out (paced so it doesn't run out early, with a fair daily share per person), and tells people when AI reading resumes. Only AI reading pauses; everything else keeps working. Modal's own spending limit is set so Modal stops rather than charging Baharul's card. A judging-week setting keeps judges from being cut off. The owner can see the remaining budget in developer mode.
24. **Respect privacy.** Phone numbers, OTPs and number plates are blurred before anything leaves your phone. Screenshots are never stored. The privacy notice says AI processing happens on Modal's servers in the USA. Ride data on the phone is deleted a day after the ride. You can see what RideMate holds and erase it from Settings, and there's a contact email for privacy requests.
25. **Owner tools.** A hidden developer mode lets the owner record what the ride apps actually show and post (masked, saved only on the phone, exported by hand) and run a capture test. The owner labels sample screens to keep the accuracy tests honest.
26. **Accurate Indian formats.** Prices like ₹1,234, Rs 1234, ₹110–130 and 1,23,456 are read correctly, including Hindi digits. Times are shown in Indian time, and the app is available in English and Hindi.
27. **Accessible and clear.** Every button has a spoken label for screen readers. Touch targets are comfortable, information is never shown by colour alone, and errors say what to do next.
28. **Hackathon-ready and release-ready.**
    - The project lives in a public GitHub repository with a clear README: what it does, what was built during the hackathon, how it uses Meta's models, "Built with Muse Spark Open", and the app's signing fingerprint.
    - It includes a script for a 3-minute demo video.
    - The installable app file is signed and published with a checksum, and the app tells users when an update is available.
29. **Tested and measured.** Automated tests cover the price checker, every ride-state change (including drivers cancelling and re-matching), the "never silent" and "always reminds" promises, attacks hidden in screen text, timeouts, limits and caps, database upgrades, and Indian number formats. An accuracy test runs on labelled sample screens without spending AI money. The owner gets alerts when something breaks, such as reading accuracy dropping after a ride app update.
